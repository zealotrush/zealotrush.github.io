This is an entry -- actually the very first entry -- from my notes.  Because it is a personal note, it lacks background information and tells jokes that only makes sense to myself.  However, I still want to put it here and hope someone would find it interesting.

---

My note-taking hopping history since I started using Emacs in 2021.

1.  org-roam (1): 2021-10 ~ 2023-08, ~23 months.

    Before org-roam, I was using [RoamResearch](https://roamresearch.com/), which made bidirectional links go viral.  I was so into it that I even bought the $500/5yrs believer plan, yet only used it for a year.

    What was wrong with it?  Well, it felt like a prototype, not a product, and certainly not one _that_ expensive.  It was full of bugs, e.g., not working well with Safari, Rime (the Chinese IME).  When you type `#define` in a C code block, a color picker would appear next to it, because `#def` could be part of a color code.  I sent feedback to the support team, and got the "thank you" response and that was it.

    Then there was org-roam, an alternative to RoamResearch in org-mode, with fewer features, fewer bugs, and no money required.  I liked it.  Bidirectional links was the only thing I needed from RoamResearch, and org-roam did it well.  There was no believer plan for org-roam, but I totally bought it.

    I was happily adding links in my notes all the time, and hoping magic beans would grow out of it someday.  It never did.  I couldn't find the notes I wanted.  The files were scattered all over the place.

    I blamed bidirectional links.  I lost faith in them.

2.  denote (1): 2023-10 ~ 2025-08, ~23 months

    I came to denote for Prot, whose name is a seal of quality.

    The journey in denote was great.  Nothing to complain about.  The one major advantage that denote has over org-roam is that I tend to write long, deep articles.  Some articles are still being linked to even after I hopped away, e.g., "Minimize clicking artifacts when adjusting audio algorithm parameters".

    So, the question is: why did I hop away anyway?  I feel ashamed to admit it: I wanted org-roam-ui.

    I was planning to develop new reverb modules at the time, and wanted to draw a mind map of the open-source reverb libraries.  I tried dot from GraphViz and it was fine, but then a thought crossed my mind: hey, why not try org-roam-ui?  I tried it, and [it does look cool](/ox-hugo/org-roam-ui-demo.mp4).  Then I hopped to org-roam again.

    Sorry, Prot!

3.  org-roam (2): 2025-08 ~ 2026-08, ~12 months

    As it turned out, org-roam-ui was only useful once.  I don't even remember how many times I have opened it, like five?

    I switched back to my add-link-crazy-mode.  Some links do work fine, like the one with "日产无麦", but most of them are just cosmetic.  I once argued that human minds work like graphs, not trees, and therefore that writing hierarchically was not the correct way to take notes -- bidirectional linking was.  I was wrong!  Human minds _are_ graphs -- that's true.  But humans read, learn, and understand things linearly and hierarchically.  That's why all textbooks and scientific papers are organized like trees, not graphs.

    I watched some Prot's videos about denote on YouTube, and decided to hop back.  Again!

    Hello, Prot!

4.  denote (2): 2026-08 ~ &lt;now&gt;

    Denote now has some cool new features, like dynamic blocks.  It's actually not a new feature at all, as it has been built into org-mode for a long time.  Denote also supports signatures, which can be used to practice the folgezettel scheme from Luhmann.  I had tried the Zettelkasten method before, without success, but that's because I was still in human-minds-are-graphs-mode.  Now, I think it's time to try it again.

    Luhmann, Prot and I, sitting in a tree!
